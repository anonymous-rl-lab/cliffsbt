# Scientific synthesis: the Covertype field

## Terminal result

Covertype provides external **distribution-level, model-conditional** support for a
decision-boundary transport mechanism. The strongest surviving statistic is boundary
density multiplied by the negative median equal-quantile signed-margin velocity.

On the last fresh target holdout (30 newly seeded MLP fits; 60 transitions), this
statistic achieved Error/Brier Spearman 0.746/0.821. Direction-specific correlations
were 0.779 and 0.746. Unsigned hidden-space distance was −0.029, and the 95th percentile
of coordinate-permuted boundaries was 0.269. The two-way source/seed cluster interval
for the robust statistic was [0.485, 0.879].

The terminal protocol nevertheless stopped at 9/10 gates: the point advantage over
ordinary mean margin transport was +0.314, but its cluster interval [−0.052, 0.678]
crossed zero. Strict robust-over-mean superiority is therefore not certified.

## What the negative experiments established

- Fifteen-metre risk derivatives were unreliable even when risk levels were reliable.
- Forty-five metres was the smallest diagnosed scale with reproducible direction and
  risk-trend sign; this is an empirical, posttarget-selected scale.
- A smooth, globally stable first/second-order geometry was not supported.
- Training-support distance was correlated in some panels but was not risk-sufficient.
- Global mean hidden displacement passed a bounded pilot and failed a new-panel formal
  confirmation.
- A shoulder-local synchronization construction failed velocity-shuffle controls and
  was rejected.
- Quantile-transport crossing coherence is degenerate under monotone unpaired coupling
  and cannot establish literal synchronization.

## Licensed claim

> In the Covertype protocol, risk changes were associated with robust signed transport
> of probability mass in the trained model's margin space, rather than with unsigned
> displacement from the training distribution alone.

## Claim boundary

Covertype windows contain different samples. They do not identify longitudinal margin
paths, first-crossing times, or sample-level crossing synchrony. Labels were used for
the scientific margin analysis, so this result is not an unlabeled deployment monitor.
The full dynamic mechanism must be tested in a paired degradation system.
