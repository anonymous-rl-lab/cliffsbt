# Editorial abstract (200 words)

Deployed classifiers can cross operational risk boundaries abruptly even as environments change gradually. We show that such cliffs can be operationally sudden but mechanistically cumulative. For a fixed deterministic classifier on paired identities, each risk increment equals correct-to-error mass minus error-to-correct mass, termed signed boundary transport (SBT). The scalar identity equals the risk increment; mechanistic information lies in the resolved ledger of incident and recovery mass, turnover, identities, path-conditioned persistence and intervention response. Paired radio-frequency and image-corruption studies reveal distributed transport localized to the focal trained boundary, while common-stream retraining reduces anchor risk and post-anchor cumulative SBT. In a preregistered CURE-OR study, an identity-anchored, confirmation-time outcome-blind readout warned 71 of 72 persistence-confirmed cliffs with three false alarms among 78 controls; guarded updates removed five model–family cliffs and introduced none. Post hoc diagnostics on disjoint identities show that simple time, risk and unsigned-shift proxies do not reproduce this operating point. Adding one outcome-blind, one-step prediction-state transport proxy to static telemetry raises timely warning from 62/72 to 71/72 without additional false alarms. Thus warning requires dynamically sufficient telemetry: ordered history can supply dynamic information, but an augmented current state can compress it. A prospective repair reversal rejects universal coverage-first selection.
