# Generalization cliffs in classifiers emerge from persistent signed boundary transport

## Abstract

Deployed classifiers can cross operational risk boundaries abruptly even as environments change gradually. We show that such cliffs can be operationally sudden but mechanistically cumulative. For a fixed deterministic classifier on paired identities, each risk increment equals correct-to-error mass minus error-to-correct mass, termed signed boundary transport (SBT). The scalar identity equals the risk increment; mechanistic information lies in the resolved ledger of incident and recovery mass, turnover, identities, path-conditioned persistence and intervention response. Paired radio-frequency and image-corruption studies reveal distributed transport localized to the focal trained boundary, while common-stream retraining reduces anchor risk and post-anchor cumulative SBT. In a preregistered CURE-OR study, an identity-anchored, confirmation-time outcome-blind readout warned 71 of 72 persistence-confirmed cliffs with three false alarms among 78 controls; guarded updates removed five model–family cliffs and introduced none. Post hoc diagnostics on disjoint identities show that simple time, risk and unsigned-shift proxies do not reproduce this operating point. Adding one outcome-blind, one-step prediction-state transport proxy to static telemetry raises timely warning from 62/72 to 71/72 without additional false alarms. Thus warning requires dynamically sufficient telemetry: ordered history can supply dynamic information, but an augmented current state can compress it. A prospective repair reversal rejects universal coverage-first selection.

## Introduction

Machine-learning systems are commonly governed by thresholded operating rules even when their environments change continuously. A classifier may remain in service while its risk stays below a declared boundary, then trigger fallback, human review, retraining or withdrawal after crossing it. Small risk changes near that boundary can therefore have disproportionately large operational consequences. We distinguish two events. The **first crossing** is the earliest state at which risk reaches the declared boundary. A **generalization cliff** is the earliest crossing that also satisfies a benchmark-specific persistence rule fixed before target evidence is interpreted. The term is operational rather than topological: it is defined relative to a fixed classifier, a deployment path, a declared boundary and a persistence functional. It does not require a discontinuous risk surface, a bifurcation or synchronized prediction collapse. Benchmark-relative boundaries can test the mechanism, but they are not automatically safety thresholds. The scientific problem is to explain how gradual change becomes an operational event, determine whether its approach is visible before outcomes arrive and identify interventions that prevent the same future deployment flow from recreating it.

Existing methods approach this problem through different proxy objects. Domain generalization and robust learning seek predictors whose performance remains stable across environments [1–5,41], while two-sample and dataset-shift tests quantify whether deployment observations differ from a reference distribution [29,30]. Neither unsigned discrepancy identifies whether probability mass moves into an error region, out of it or approximately tangent to the learned decision boundary. Unsupervised accuracy estimation, calibration and selective prediction estimate current reliability from confidence, agreement or prediction structure [6–8,31–34], but current risk is a position rather than an account of the transitions that produced its change or the headroom that remains. Sequential monitors and change-point methods detect harmful shifts or violations as outcomes or proxies accumulate [9–12,43,44], but do not by themselves identify the task-specific motion generating the risk increment or guarantee advance warning. Generic early-warning signals target signatures of critical transitions [13–15], whereas a model-risk boundary can be crossed without a loss of dynamical stability. Finally, test-time adaptation, active learning, influence estimation and data valuation alter the model or prioritize examples [35–40], but lack a common dynamical target that distinguishes filling an uncovered deployment region from repeatedly sampling the same boundary fragment.

Several neighbouring concepts track prediction changes along different axes. Prediction churn compares successive model versions on common inputs [45]; example-forgetting events follow training examples as a model changes over optimization epochs [46]; and performance-shift attribution decomposes an aggregate performance difference across changes in data-generating mechanisms [47]. Here the trained model is held fixed and the same deployment identities are followed through changing states. Direction is retained: correct-to-error and error-to-correct transitions are separated rather than collapsed into an unsigned flip count. What is missing from the deployment literature is therefore a signed, task-specific object connecting environmental motion to changes in prediction state and thereby linking cliff formation, warning and training intervention.

Consider a fixed deterministic classifier evaluated on the same identities at consecutive deployment states. Let \(R_t\) be empirical misclassification risk, \(J_t^+\) the mass changing from correct to error and \(J_t^-\) the mass changing from error to correct. Then

\[
R_{t+1}-R_t
=
J_t^+-J_t^-
\equiv
\operatorname{SBT}_t .
\tag{1}
\]

We call this signed boundary transport. The equality is deliberately simple and contains no more scalar information than \(\Delta R_t\). The substantive object is the **resolved transport ledger**: incident and recovery mass separately, gross turnover, the identities and boundary fragments involved, first-crossing times, persistence along the declared path and response to intervention. Two transitions can have the same net risk increment but different turnover, cancellation and susceptible mass, with different implications for recovery and data acquisition. The transport sequence is defined before an operational boundary is chosen. For boundary \(\beta\), ordinary first crossing is

\[
\tau^{\times}_\beta
=
\inf\left\{T:
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\operatorname{SBT}_t
\ge \beta-R_0
\right\},
\tag{2a}
\]

whereas the operational cliff is

\[
\tau_{\beta,\mathcal P}
=
\inf\left\{T\ge\tau^{\times}_\beta:
\mathcal P_\beta(R_{T:T+q})=1
\right\},
\tag{2b}
\]

with \(\mathcal P_\beta\) the prespecified persistence functional. Cumulative SBT determines the threshold crossing exactly; persistence confirms that the crossing is operationally retained under the benchmark rule. In the title, **persistent harmful transport** means a sustained positive net imbalance over the declared analysis scale. It does not require every incremental SBT value to be positive, nor does it imply that identity persistence is independent of path geometry. A large incident flow need not create a cliff when recovery cancels it; small positive increments can create one through sustained imbalance; and identities need not cross together. Thus a cliff can be operationally abrupt while its formation remains cumulative.

SBT places formation, warning and control on one axis while keeping their evidential status distinct. Formation is exact paired accounting, promoted to mechanism evidence only when the resolved ledger is temporally distributed, preferentially organized around the focal trained boundary, persistent under the declared path and altered by an upstream intervention. Under additional smoothness, SBT admits a continuous boundary-current representation and local risk susceptibilities summarized by a gradient \(b\) and curvature \(H\); these are conditional charts rather than assumptions required by Eq. (1). Warning is a partial-observation problem. Outcome-blind telemetry—defined here as telemetry excluding contemporaneous target labels during deployment, although labelled calibration may be used—must preserve a risk-relevant state. In CURE-OR, the online departure and return channels are transitions relative to each identity's baseline predicted class, not true correct-to-error and error-to-correct events. We denote their signed difference by \(\widetilde{\operatorname{SBT}}_t\), an outcome-blind prediction-state transport proxy rather than exact task-error SBT. Ordered history can add information about future cumulative SBT, but history and current state are substitutable only to the extent that an augmented state already encodes recent transport. We call this requirement **dynamic sufficiency**. Control acts upstream because changing training support changes the learned boundary and therefore the transport induced by the same deployment stream. The allocation of a limited repair budget remains less settled: a prospective reversal rejects universal coverage-first selection, while support density, model class and budget remain confounded as possible moderators.

We test this framework through complementary interventions rather than a single aggregate benchmark score. A controlled radio-frequency field generated with TorchSig [17,18] preserves signal identity along ordered deployment paths, enabling exact incident–recovery accounting, temporal-order intervention, common-stream retraining and random-boundary placebos. We transfer paired formation to three independently trained convolutional networks evaluated on all 15 CIFAR-10-C corruptions, five severities and 10,000 shared identities [24,25]. A fixed incorrect-label partition and a post hoc closest-risk trained-peer analysis provide complementary task-agnostic and learned-boundary nulls. We prospectively test—and falsify—the universal extension of a coverage-first repair rule. Finally, a preregistered two-phase CURE-OR study [27,28] evaluates formation, warning and calibration-gated updating on five held-out classifier-head seeds sharing one frozen representation. Additional analyses reuse only committed outputs. Their questions were specified after confirmation reveal, so they are post hoc diagnostics even when fitting and threshold selection use calibration identities and evaluation uses disjoint confirmation identities. They compare warning proxies, stratify event difficulty, examine classifier-head clustering, vary false-alarm budgets, compare focal and trained-peer boundaries, vary operational thresholds without changing the ledger and decompose retraining gains into anchor improvement and future SBT suppression.

Our contributions are fourfold.

1. **Exact accounting with resolved mechanistic content.** We introduce SBT for fixed deterministic classifiers on paired identities, distinguish ordinary first crossing from a persistence-confirmed cliff and separate the scalar identity from the richer incident–recovery ledger. Complementary random, incorrect and closest-risk trained-peer placebos test whether the observed organization is localized to the focal model boundary.
2. **A conditional account of observation and warning.** Temporal-order intervention identifies a prospective history component in the controlled radio-frequency field. In CURE-OR, post hoc matched-calibration diagnostics on disjoint confirmation identities show that simple time, risk and generic shift proxies do not reproduce the registered operating point. A strictly nested analysis identifies one-step net prediction-state transport as the channel accounting for the observed improvement over a position-like telemetry state, motivating a formal dynamic-sufficiency criterion rather than a universal requirement for long histories.
3. **Training-support control of future transport.** Equal-size common-stream interventions show that support restoration reshapes the learned boundary and reduces post-anchor cumulative SBT. An exact decomposition separates this effect from improved starting accuracy.
4. **A calibrated evidence chain with explicit boundaries.** Paired radio-frequency and image studies, a prospective repair-law falsification and a preregistered same-benchmark serial loop establish what is exact, what is path- and calibration-conditional, what is intervention-supported and what remains open at the representation, natural-deployment and allocation levels.

## Results

### Persistent resolved boundary transport forms cliffs under ordered challenge paths

For a fixed classifier, we distinguish first threshold crossing from a persistence-confirmed generalization cliff as in Eqs. (2a–b). To determine how that aggregate event is assembled, we followed the same deployment identities through ordered challenge states and recorded whether each identity remained correct, changed from correct to error or recovered. Because scalar SBT equals the risk increment exactly, the non-trivial empirical questions concern the **resolved ledger**: how much incident and recovery flow occurs, when and where identities cross, whether crossings remain in error along the declared path, whether they are organized around the focal trained boundary and whether training changes them. The principal challenge ladders increase impairment or corruption severity; endpoint persistence is therefore a property of the model–path pair, not a path-independent constant of the classifier (Fig. 1).

We first studied a controlled radio-frequency classification field generated with TorchSig 2.1.1 [17,18]. Five fresh training seeds produced paired baseline and intervention models. Each model was evaluated on the same 512 latent signals along two degradation paths; symbol draws and stochastic phase-noise and additive-noise variates were preserved across windows, while only the deployment condition changed. This identity-preserving design decomposed every risk increment without imposing a coupling between unrelated batches.

A stronger initial hypothesis predicted a synchronized pulse: boundary-near samples would acquire unusually harmful velocity and cross together. The data rejected this account. None of the ten baseline model–path pairs showed additional position–velocity coupling beyond a class-conditional shuffle comparator. Instead, all ten crossed the common protocol boundary through temporally distributed net transport. Correct-to-error transitions minus recoveries reconstructed every risk increment with zero numerical discrepancy. The median endpoint persistence of crossing identities was 0.9525, normalized first-crossing entropy was 0.8789 and the largest three-window interval contained only 0.3827 of incident transitions. The operational event therefore emerged from small groups crossing at different times rather than from one failure pulse. Because these paths were ordered degradations, the high endpoint persistence supports sustained accumulation on those paths but does not by itself establish persistence under a dwell–reversal trajectory.

The observed organization was preferentially localized to the trained task boundary. In nine of ten baseline model–path pairs, peak correct-to-error transport through the focal boundary exceeded the 95th percentile from matched random boundaries in probability space. The class-conditional velocity shuffle, by contrast, did not reveal the synchronized acceleration predicted by the rejected mechanism. These controls separate exact bookkeeping from the empirical claim, but random partitions are a weak null because they are not learned decision boundaries.

We therefore tested a stronger trained-peer placebo using committed predictions. For each focal model–path, we selected the other seed or classifier head with the closest anchor risk and used that peer model’s incident-minus-recovery ledger to reconstruct the focal model’s risk increments. Exact focal closure is guaranteed by Eq. (1), so the informative quantity is the magnitude of peer mismatch rather than a self-pass/peer-fail label. Across all 45 CIFAR-10-C seed–corruption cells, mean reconstruction RMSE was 0.07487 for the fixed incorrect partition and 0.01539 for the closest-risk trained peer, compared with numerical zero for the focal ledger. The trained-peer error was not negligible relative to the process being reconstructed: its median ratio to the focal cell’s root-mean-square risk increment was 0.165 (0.164 in cliff cells), despite a median anchor-risk gap of only 0.0002. In CURE-OR, closest-risk peers produced mean RMSE 0.02750 over active paths and 0.03433 over cliff paths, with median normalized errors of 0.858 and 0.707, respectively, despite a median anchor-risk gap of 0.02. The incorrect partition and closest-risk peer are complementary nulls rather than a universally controlled ladder: the former is task agnostic, whereas the latter is a learned boundary with similar anchor risk. Peer mismatch was larger relative to focal increments in CURE-OR than in CIFAR-10-C, but anchor-risk separation, increment scale and head parameterization change simultaneously across the two fields, so the cause of that cross-domain contrast remains unresolved (Fig. 2d; Extended Data Fig. 2).

We next evaluated the same formation account in an official image corruption benchmark. Three independently trained convolutional networks were applied to all 15 CIFAR-10-C corruption families, five ordered severities and the same 10,000 identities [24,25]. Mean endpoint risk increased by 0.26858, with a descriptive three-seed cluster range of [0.25089, 0.27885]. Correct-to-error transitions minus recoveries closed every adjacent-severity risk increment, with maximum error \(9.72\times10^{-17}\). Median endpoint persistence was 0.88056 [0.87349, 0.88543], and normalized first-crossing entropy was 0.91901 [0.90417, 0.93170]. The prespecified incorrect partition assigned each identity a fixed wrong class by adding a deterministic non-zero random offset to its true label; using transitions through this fixed partition increased reconstruction RMSE by 0.07487 [0.06846, 0.07882] relative to the focal task boundary.

Within-domain controls showed why increasing risk is not itself a cliff. Every corruption family raised endpoint risk for all three networks, but only 11 exhausted the common 0.15 risk headroom for every seed. Pixelation crossed for two seeds, whereas brightness, elastic transformation and JPEG compression crossed for none. Glass blur, snow and elastic transformation all produced substantial recovery; only glass blur and snow retained enough positive incident-minus-recovery balance to exhaust headroom. Summed recovery was approximately 0.42 for glass blur, 0.41–0.44 for snow and 0.41–0.42 for elastic transformation, illustrating that monotone severity does not mechanically forbid recovery. Nevertheless, the absence of a non-monotone reversal arm means that endpoint persistence remains conditioned on the ordered challenge geometry. Prior true-margin separation was 1.23488 [1.16775, 1.27827], supporting the interpretation that identities approached the learned boundary before crossing.

Operational thresholds changed first-passage incidence and timing but not the underlying ledger. Recomputing the frozen TorchSig trajectories across boundaries from 0.15 to 0.35 left all ten support-depleted paths crossing and all ten random-broad and Cliff-aware paths non-crossing; baseline incidence declined from ten to six as the boundary moved above some endpoints. This post hoc sensitivity analysis supports the intervention ordering without assigning safety meaning to alternative lines (Fig. 2; Extended Data Figs. 1a and 2). Complete qualification histories, placebos and sensitivity tables are reported in Supplementary Notes 2, 4, 6 and 8.

### Advance warning requires dynamically sufficient telemetry; explicit history is conditionally useful

Exact retrospective accounting does not imply advance warning. Outcomes defining SBT arrive after the transition, whereas a deployed alarm must operate from pre-outcome telemetry. We therefore asked whether telemetry preserves enough information about current risk position and future headroom depletion, and when an ordered history contributes beyond the current state.

A fresh TorchSig source used a frozen 25-dimensional telemetry channel, a six-window history and a five-window horizon. A local chart estimated current risk, and a linear trend over the latest six windows supplied the forecast. Across two independently feasible degradation directions, the position-plus-velocity rule warned 62.5% of persistent crossings within the designated horizon, compared with 5.83% for contemporaneous risk detection. Stationary false alarms were 4.17%, and median timely lead was three windows. A generic entropy–margin trend warned only 1.11% of events and alarmed on every stationary control in an earlier frozen replay. Because that earlier comparator did not receive an independently calibrated false-alarm budget, it is retained as a diagnostic failure rather than treated as a fair competitive baseline.

To isolate the contribution of temporal order, we permuted pre-crossing histories while preserving their complete value multiset and terminal pre-crossing state. Each of 120 event replicates generated 240 matched counterfactual histories, for 28,800 replays. Destroying order reduced mean integrated slope by 0.005398 [0.004982, 0.005815] and integrated forecast by 0.030075 [0.027460, 0.032763]. Ordered histories produced the larger forecast in 98.33% of matched replicates, and positive slopes occurred in 92.83% of ordered decision windows versus 55.81% after shuffling. The order-specific component accounted for 85.4% of the ordered trend magnitude. Alarms did not vanish because the elevated terminal position was deliberately retained; the continuous slope and forecast contrasts, rather than zero alarms, identify how this frozen forecasting functional uses temporal organization. The shuffled histories need not be physically realizable, and the result does not imply that every cliff has a gradual precursor (Fig. 3a).

The initial implementation reconstructed named TorchSig impairment coordinates before estimating risk. A separate replay trained charts directly from batch telemetry and withheld those coordinates online. The best direct chart, using 25 telemetry means and log variances, achieved 72.5% timely warning, no premature or stationary false alarms and median lead two. A mean-only version achieved 70.0%; an oracle receiving the physical coordinates achieved 77.5% but generated more premature alarms. A wrong-reference placebo reduced timely warning to zero and increased position error to 0.159031. Conversely, a training-relative representation did not outperform an equally expressive reference-free chart: its timely-warning difference was \(-0.00833\), with interval [\(-0.025\), 0], and its position-error difference also spanned zero.

These experiments distinguish current-risk observability, dynamic sufficiency and operational warnability. TorchSig identifies an ordered-history contribution for one controlled local-drift regime. The CURE-OR comparison below shows that this contribution is not universally necessary: when a current telemetry state already contains task-oriented departure, margin and one-step transition summaries, a refitted chart can approach the registered temporal detector. Warning is therefore telemetry-, path-, calibration- and horizon-conditional rather than governed by one universal history rule. Formal observability conditions and complete warning comparisons are given in Supplementary Notes 1, 2, 7 and 8.

### Support restoration suppresses future transport beyond anchor-risk gains

We next treated training data as an upstream control rather than a fixed reference. Four equal-size training-support designs were crossed with five fresh seeds, producing 20 ExtraTrees models. Total training size, class balance, learner specification and within-seed model randomness were held fixed, and every model was evaluated on the same 32,000-example calibration cache and 84,000-example deployment cache.

The support-depleted arm sampled a narrow source region; the baseline used the original source support. Random-broad and Cliff-aware arms each replaced 5% of the 2,000-example training set. Random-broad enrichment sampled across the controlled deployment cube, whereas Cliff-aware enrichment concentrated the same 100-example budget around two frozen deployment paths. A common boundary inherited from an independent protocol was used for all models.

Training support qualitatively changed the future trajectories. Mean endpoint risk was 0.3936 after support depletion and 0.3341 at baseline. All ten baseline seed–direction paths crossed. Random-broad enrichment reduced endpoint risk to 0.0130 and Cliff-aware enrichment to 0.0010; neither enriched arm crossed on any path. Path-risk areas were 0.2938, 0.2401, 0.00945 and 0.00110 for support-depleted, baseline, random-broad and Cliff-aware training, respectively.

Relative to baseline, Cliff-aware enrichment reduced terminal risk by 0.33310, with a descriptive five-seed cluster range of [0.29045, 0.36830], and reduced path-risk area by 0.23899 [0.20198, 0.27026]. Random-broad enrichment produced almost the same dominant effect. The targeted arm provided a smaller additional advantage over random broad: terminal risk was lower by 0.01200 [0.00350, 0.02625], and path-risk area by 0.00835 [0.00265, 0.01745]. All five seed-level contrasts favoured Cliff-aware training, corresponding to an exact one-sided sign tail of 1/32, but the principal result is support restoration rather than universal superiority of targeting. Both enriched arms moved the field close to the risk floor, so binary crossing saturated and the targeted comparison was evaluated through predeclared continuous endpoints. The magnitude hierarchy is important: restoration of broad support produced the dominant improvement, while path-targeted placement supplied a smaller, reproducible increment.

The intervention changed more than crossing time. Cliff-aware enrichment reduced the local risk-gradient norm from 1.055517 to 0.005255 and the Hessian norm from 2.420089 to 0.092255, leaving a nearly flat low-risk field over the tested domain. Anchor risk declined from 0.219891 to 0.001220. The observation-information trace also changed, from 155.7854 to 134.7827, showing that training altered observation geometry along with decision geometry; the decrease is not itself an improvement because normalized risk directions become unstable when risk variation nearly vanishes.

A separate paired experiment resolved sample-level transport after retraining. On new latent streams, Cliff-aware training reduced terminal risk by 0.27285 [0.21543, 0.33496] and incident correct-to-error crossing by 0.12148 [0.08574, 0.15176]. The common-stream ledger further decomposed terminal improvement into two exact components:

\[
R_W(T)-R_{W_S}(T)
=
\bigl[R_W(0)-R_{W_S}(0)\bigr]
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\bigl[\operatorname{SBT}_{W,t}-\operatorname{SBT}_{W_S,t}\bigr].
\tag{3}
\]

For Cliff-aware versus baseline training, the 0.33310 terminal gain comprised 0.17455 anchor-risk improvement and 0.15855 post-anchor cumulative-SBT suppression. Random-broad enrichment decomposed similarly: 0.32110 terminal gain comprised 0.16715 anchor improvement and 0.15395 future suppression. Future SBT suppression was positive in every seed–path comparison for both enriched arms. Thus approximately half of the large enrichment effect occurred after the deployment anchor, while the additional targeted advantage over broad restoration remained modest (Fig. 4). Full geometry, paired ledgers and decompositions are in Supplementary Notes 2, 4 and 8.

### A cross-domain reversal rules out a universal repair-allocation rule

Showing that training support controls future transport does not determine how a limited repair budget should be allocated. A high local-hazard score identifies an active crossing, but several such examples can be redundant if they represent the same class, phase or boundary fragment. We therefore compared support coverage with hazard concentration under equal budgets.

In a sparse 16-cell radio-frequency field, five fresh training seeds shared one acquisition pool per seed. Hazard concentration achieved a 78.75% true-incident hit rate but covered only 43.75% of cells. Coverage-random covered every cell while hitting only 7.50% of local incidents. Despite selecting fewer acute events, coverage-random reduced terminal risk by 0.24609 versus 0.15195 for hazard concentration, a difference of 0.09414 with descriptive five-seed range [0.03633, 0.14648]. Four of five seed-level contrasts were positive. Once every cell was represented, adding within-cell hazard ranking changed the mean reduction by only 0.00039. This controlled field therefore showed that repeatedly selecting the deepest local events can be inferior to filling consequential support gaps.

We prospectively tested the stronger coverage-first claim in CIFAR-10-C. Coverage and hazard arms each selected 1,000 ensemble-confirmed first crossings and each represented all 15 corruption families, ten classes and five severities. Coverage expanded occupied corruption-by-class-by-first-severity fragments from 262 to 731, whereas hazard selected the deepest negative margins. The ordering reversed: endpoint error was 0.55494 under coverage and 0.54172 under hazard. Coverage minus hazard endpoint error was +0.01322 [0.01041, 0.01611], and the risk-area difference was +0.00846 [0.00645, 0.00993], with the same direction in all three fresh seeds. Coverage still improved on the clean-only baseline by 0.04741 [0.03596, 0.05912] and outperformed random augmentation by 0.00763 [0.00697, 0.00882], but it did not beat hazard once both arms already had broad domain support.

The two studies therefore falsify a universal coverage-first rule rather than identify its moderator. Support density is a plausible explanation for the reversal, but domain, model class and budget also changed between the radio-frequency and image studies. The proposed sequence—fill major support gaps before pressure, exposure, redundancy and transfer guide later allocations—remains a hypothesis requiring a prospective factorial manipulation of coverage and pressure (Fig. 5). Complete selector failures, manipulations and the retained `PARTIAL_OR_STOP` decision are reported in Supplementary Notes 5 and 6.

### A preregistered serial test confirms formation, warning and guarded updating

The preceding studies isolated formation, observation and control in complementary settings. CURE-OR v2 tested whether they could operate serially on one frozen benchmark field with held-out classifier-head seeds and a preregistered decision rule [27,28]. A frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny representation supported five ridge heads trained with seeds 113, 127, 139, 151 and 163. Fifty class-balanced identities were reserved for calibration and a disjoint 50 for confirmation. Ten challenge families were traversed under three asynchronous schedules over 13 windows, producing 150 model–schedule–family paths. The five classifier-head seeds—not paths, families or schedules—were the inferential units.

The study used two phases. Phase 1 fitted heads, generated confirmation predictions, recorded outcome-blind telemetry, committed warning scores and applied the calibration repair gate without scoring confirmation labels. Phase 2 verified the commitment before reading truth and evaluating the registered formation, warning and repair modules.

Formation closed on all 150 eligible paths. Seventy-two formed persistent cliffs and 78 were controls; every classifier-head seed contributed at least one cliff. Correct-to-error counts minus recoveries reconstructed every risk increment with maximum absolute error \(1.11\times10^{-16}\). Median endpoint-minus-baseline risk on cliff paths was 0.54; this quantity is not the endpoint overshoot above the 0.50 boundary.

The frozen Hybrid25 readout warned 71 of 72 persistence-confirmed cliffs and raised three false alarms among 78 controls, with median lead three windows. It was calibrated using labelled calibration data but received no confirmation labels or challenge-severity values during deployment. The channel was nevertheless **identity anchored**: departures and returns were computed relative to each tracked identity's baseline predicted class. Its one-step net channel is therefore an outcome-blind prediction-state transport proxy \(\widetilde{\operatorname{SBT}}_t\), not the exact task-error SBT of Eq. (1). The pooled false-alarm field rate was 3/78; the seed-level rates were 0, 3/18, 0, 0 and 0, yielding a descriptive seed-cluster bootstrap range of 0–10.7%. Thus the 78 controls characterize this frozen benchmark field, while uncertainty about model-level false-alarm behavior is governed by five head seeds.

We then performed a post hoc matched-calibration comparison using only committed feature and prediction arrays. Each baseline was fitted on the 50 calibration identities, with regularization selected by leave-one-family-out calibration; its alarm threshold was chosen to maximize timely warning while keeping calibration false alarms at or below 7.5%, matching the registered calibration diagnostic. The fitted rule was applied without parameter or threshold tuning on the disjoint confirmation identities. However, the analysis questions and feature-set comparisons were specified after confirmation reveal. These results are therefore post hoc diagnostics evaluated on identities disjoint from fitting, not an independent held-out confirmation or an algorithm-ranking study.

The complete table changes the interpretation in two ways. First, refitting the full 34-input temporal chart under the matched calibration budget produced 61/72 timely warnings with 1/78 false alarms, whereas the registered detector produced 71/72 with 3/78; no utility function was prespecified to rank that sensitivity–specificity trade-off, and the comparison does not estimate architecture-level superiority between the registered and refitted readouts. Second, the one-step augmented chart approached the registered operating point, so we performed a strictly nested channel analysis. Starting from the same nine static channels, adding only one-step net departure minus return increased timely warning from 62/72 to 71/72 at the unchanged 3/78 false alarms. Adding persistent departure alone left performance at 62/72, while adding both proxy channels yielded 70/72. Differences of one event among the leading rules lie within the single-event resolution of this benchmark and are not interpreted as evidence of superiority, equivalence or harm. The net-proxy gain over static telemetry was positive in four of five classifier-head seeds, with seedwise timely-event increments of 3, 1, 0, 1 and 4 and a descriptive complete-seed bootstrap range of 0.042–0.213 for the pooled timely-rate difference. With the fitted models fixed and thresholds recalibrated on calibration scores, the advantage of the net-proxy state over static telemetry persisted across false-alarm budgets from 5% to 20%, with the expected sensitivity–specificity trade-off (Extended Data Figs. 3 and 5). These post hoc diagnostics, evaluated on confirmation identities disjoint from fitting, indicate that one outcome-blind prediction-state transport proxy adds warning value beyond a position-like task state in this field; they do not constitute prospective confirmation of universal ledger superiority. Explicit multi-window history is therefore not uniquely necessary when an augmented state is dynamically sufficient. The strongest intervention evidence that temporal order itself can add information remains the controlled TorchSig experiment (Fig. 3b,c).

The higher confirmation rate than the calibration diagnostic (59/69 timely, 6/81 false) was not explained solely by deep endpoint cliffs. Using calibration-defined endpoint-overshoot tertiles, the registered detector warned 27/27 confirmation cliffs in the shallow stratum (endpoint overshoot at most 0.12), 32/33 in the middle stratum and 12/12 in the upper stratum. It also warned 28/29 events occurring by window 3. Across calibration-defined pre-event-slope strata, it warned 10/10 low-slope, 24/24 middle-slope and 37/38 high-slope events. Confirmation cliffs nevertheless occupied a different regime: median initial headroom was 0.22 versus 0.14 in calibration, and median pre-event risk slope was 0.06 versus 0.03. The sole missed confirmation event occurred at window 3 and was not shallow. These diagnostics show strong performance across the observed overshoot and onset strata while preserving the limitation that no minimum-signal or minimum-population warnability boundary was estimated (Fig. 3d; Extended Data Fig. 4).

The guarded update used a fixed 500-example family-by-class coverage set. A candidate delta-ridge head was deployed only if calibration removed at least one family-level cliff, introduced none, increased no calibration path’s mean risk and met the prespecified aggregate gain. Four of five heads passed. Their updates removed five model–family cliffs, introduced none and gave every eligible seed at least one removed cliff. Seed 127 failed the no-harm criterion and retained its baseline head; its zero effect remained in the analysis. Across all five seed clusters, mean deployment-risk gain was 0.06203 with registered interval [0.02981, 0.08524], and mean event-time gain was 1.14 windows [0.493, 1.700]. Alternative-boundary diagnostics showed that the registered detector was calibrated rather than threshold invariant; the transport ledger remained exact because only first-passage labels changed.

This result is a same-benchmark confirmation on held-out classifier-head seeds, not representation-level replication: all five heads share one frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone. Hybrid25 is confirmation-time outcome blind but not end-to-end label free; labels orient calibration and repair, and recurrent identity anchors are required to define departure from each baseline prediction. The 50-identity decision unit was fixed by the preregistered class-balanced split rather than selected from a population-size curve. The update confirms one guarded intervention and abstention rule, not a universal acquisition policy (Fig. 6; Extended Data Fig. 1b). The preregistration, comparative diagnostics, difficulty strata and scope boundaries are given in Supplementary Notes 7 and 8.

## Discussion

### SBT resolves deployment structure hidden by scalar risk

This work reframes an operational generalization cliff as a transport problem rather than an extreme value of distributional distance. A population can move far from its training distribution while remaining within regions of unchanged prediction state, whereas a smaller displacement can move dense probability mass across the learned task boundary. Unsigned shift therefore cannot determine risk change without the classifier, direction of motion and recovery flow.

The scalar SBT identity is exact but deliberately modest: it is the risk increment. Its scientific value comes from resolving the ledger that the scalar curve hides. For example, \((J_t^+,J_t^-)=(0.10,0)\) and \((0.40,0.30)\) produce the same SBT but imply different turnover, recovery and susceptible mass. Prediction churn instead measures disagreement between model versions on a common input distribution [45], and example forgetting follows a changing model during training [46]. SBT fixes the realized model and follows deployment identities as their states change; performance-shift attribution addresses which generating mechanisms contribute to an aggregate performance change [47], whereas the resolved ledger identifies which identities cross, in which direction and with what recovery. These objects are complementary rather than interchangeable.

The paired experiments add timing, path-conditioned persistence, fragment structure and intervention response. Random and incorrect partitions establish focal-boundary locality against task-agnostic nulls; the closest-risk peer-model analysis adds a learned-boundary null. The placebos are complementary rather than a strictly controlled universal gradient, and focal self-closure is only the zero reference guaranteed by identity. Closure without resolved structure is bookkeeping, while aggregate improvement without paired transport cannot distinguish a better anchor from suppression of future harmful flow.

The framework also separates a boundary-independent mechanism from a boundary-relative event. The ledger is unchanged when \(\beta\) changes; the boundary specifies headroom and ordinary first crossing, and a prespecified persistence functional determines when that crossing qualifies as an operational cliff. The principal TorchSig, CIFAR-10-C and CURE-OR challenges are ordered severity ladders. Non-negligible recovery demonstrates that monotone challenge does not mechanically enforce one-way identity transitions, but the high endpoint-persistence values cannot be promoted to a path-independent classifier property without a dwell–reversal experiment. The supported claim is sustained net accumulation under the tested ordered paths.

### Formation, observation and control require different evidence

Formation has the strongest mathematical status. Equation (1) holds for fixed deterministic classifiers on paired identities without smoothness or architectural assumptions, and it extends to fixed non-negative identity or class weights. Its mechanism-qualified interpretation in the tested fields relies on resolved turnover, distributed timing, focal-boundary locality and intervention response. The continuous current and local coefficients \(b\) and \(H\) occupy a conditional level: they summarize a smooth local deployment family. The experimental severity ladders are discrete and are not assumed to satisfy the continuity equation; the current representation is a theoretical interpolation, not the estimator used for paired accounting.

Warning is more restrictive. Retrospective SBT does not reveal future transport before outcomes arrive. TorchSig shows that temporal order contributes to one frozen forecasting functional after its state inventory and terminal pre-crossing value are controlled. CURE-OR confirms that identity-anchored, task-oriented telemetry can warn on one preregistered field. Its online one-step net-departure channel is \(\widetilde{\operatorname{SBT}}\), a prediction-state proxy defined without target labels, rather than exact task-error SBT. Post hoc matched-calibration diagnostics on disjoint confirmation identities show that time, estimated risk and generic unsigned-shift proxies do not reproduce the registered operating point. They do not constitute a second confirmatory study, because the comparison questions were specified after confirmation reveal.

The nested analysis motivates a sharper observation principle. Let an augmented state \(S_t\) contain current telemetry and an outcome-blind summary of the transition from \(t-1\) to \(t\). The state is dynamically sufficient for horizon \(L\) when earlier history contains no additional information about the future persistence-confirmed event after conditioning on \(S_t\) and the calibration context. In CURE-OR, adding the one-step net prediction-state transport proxy accounted for the observed increase over static telemetry, while persistence alone did not. The gain was distributed across four of five classifier-head seeds. This does not prove the conditional-independence definition or make long history universally irrelevant; it shows that an augmented state can compress part of the dynamic information that TorchSig obtains from ordered history. Differences of one event among the leading CURE-OR rules are not interpreted as performance ordering.

Control is interventional but domain bounded. Training support changed the learned boundary, local risk geometry, anchor risk and post-anchor cumulative SBT on common streams. The dominant result was restoration of broad support; Cliff-aware targeting provided a smaller additional advantage. Repair allocation remains the least settled layer. The radio-frequency and image studies falsify universal coverage-first selection but do not identify support density as the moderator because domain, model class and budget co-vary. A coverage-by-pressure factorial remains necessary.

### Implications for deployment monitoring and data-centric control

A deployment audit should report more than a scalar shift score. A minimal operational state includes estimated risk headroom, a task-oriented transport coordinate or proxy, recent transition summaries, the calibration domain, the forecast horizon and the effective population represented by each decision unit. In the CURE-OR diagnostic comparison, estimated current risk warned 28/72 cliffs, while generic entropy–margin and unsigned-shift rules transferred with 20/78 and 21/78 false alarms. Static task-oriented telemetry warned 62/72; adding the one-step net prediction-state transport proxy raised this to 71/72 at the same 3/78 false alarms. The comparison is post hoc and identity anchored, but it is the first direct diagnostic evidence in the paper that a transport-oriented summary can add warning value beyond a position-like state.

Direct identity pairing will not always be available. The paired experiments nevertheless define what a surrogate should approximate: margin or prediction-state flow should reconstruct risk change, distinguish the focal boundary from trained peers and remain informative under intervention. An online channel may use baseline-prediction transitions as a proxy, but it must not be conflated with exact outcome-defined SBT. In populations without recurring identities, an explicit coupling or distribution-level current estimator is required.

Training data should likewise be treated as a control input, not merely as the reference from which deployment departs. Repair studies should hold data budget and deployment streams fixed, retrain the model and remeasure both anchor risk and subsequent transport. Guarded deployment is equally important. The CURE-OR update was applied only after calibration benefit and no-harm checks; one head retained its baseline. Abstention is an intended output when observation or intervention evidence is insufficient.

### Scope and next tests

The flagship CURE-OR protocol provides head-level prospective replication conditional on one frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny representation. Its five classifier-head seeds are genuine inferential units for head variability, but representation-level independence is \(n=1\). This is the principal model-level external-validity boundary and motivates independent backbones before broad architectural claims. The official CIFAR study adds three independently trained small CNNs, but it remains an ordered corruption benchmark rather than a natural deployment stream.

The strongest formation results require paired identities and fixed deterministic zero–one classifiers. Natural deployments with changing populations require an explicit coupling or probability-current estimator; adaptive or stochastic models require an expanded state accounting for boundary motion. The absence of non-monotone dwell–reversal paths prevents path-independent persistence claims. The 50-identity CURE-OR unit was fixed by preregistration rather than a scale experiment, so the study establishes warnability at that unit but neither a minimum effective population nor a universal false-alarm rate. The pooled 3/78 false-alarm rate corresponds to a seed-cluster descriptive range of 0–10.7% because all three alarms occurred in one seed–family cell.

The committed-output analyses reduce earlier ambiguities but retain their temporal status. Shallow endpoint overshoot did not explain the confirmation advantage; closest-risk peers failed to reconstruct focal increments; and the one-step proxy gain persisted across classifier-head seeds and a range of calibration false-alarm budgets. These analyses remain post hoc. The next decisive tests are a preregistered comparison of position-only, net-proxy, persistence-proxy and full-history states on fresh physical systems; non-monotone dwell–reversal paths; independent representations; direct prediction of recovery or repair value from the resolved ledger; and a coverage-by-pressure factorial. A real physical, temporally ordered cross-embodiment protocol has been developed for these tests, but it is not part of the evidence reported here unless independently frozen and completed before revision.

The broader implication is that the exact accounting layer may transfer before a telemetry chart, persistence regime or repair policy does. Partial transfer across new systems would refine rather than invalidate the hierarchy. The relevant deployment questions are how probability mass changes task state relative to a boundary shaped by training, whether the available observation is dynamically sufficient and whether the boundary can be changed before cumulative harmful transport exhausts the remaining headroom.

## Methods

### Study design and evidence levels

The study separated formation, warning, control and repair allocation. Formation used identity-paired outcome transitions. Warning used telemetry that excluded contemporaneous target labels during deployment, although labelled calibration was permitted. Control changed training support under equal data budgets and replayed models on common streams. Allocation compared equal-size repair sets. Exact accounting has the strongest status; smooth local geometry is conditional; warning depends on telemetry, calibration, horizon and population; control is intervention-supported within tested fields; and allocation remains empirically bounded.

The experimental programme comprised TorchSig, Covertype, CIFAR-10-C and CURE-OR [17,18,23–25,27,28]. Protocols, seeds, primary contrasts, stopping rules and invalidation conditions were fixed before designated target runs. Additional v7 analyses reuse committed predictions and telemetry and are explicitly post hoc. Supplementary Notes 1–10 contain proofs, qualification stages, negative results, complete tables and the artifact map.

### Signed boundary transport and cliff definitions

For fixed deterministic classifier \(W\), let \(e_{i,t}=1\) when identity \(i\) is misclassified at deployment state \(t\), and zero otherwise. For fixed non-negative identity weights \(w_i\) summing to one,

\[
R_t^w=\sum_i w_i e_{i,t}.
\]

Incident and recovery mass are

\[
J_{t,w}^+=\sum_i w_i\mathbf 1\{e_{i,t}=0,e_{i,t+1}=1\},
\qquad
J_{t,w}^-=\sum_i w_i\mathbf 1\{e_{i,t}=1,e_{i,t+1}=0\}.
\]

Then \(R_{t+1}^w-R_t^w=J_{t,w}^+-J_{t,w}^-\) exactly. Uniform \(w_i=1/N\) gives the empirical risk used in the paired studies; fixed class-balanced or cost-sensitive weights give the same identity. The resolved ledger additionally records gross turnover, incident and recovery identity sets, declared boundary fragments, first-crossing times and endpoint persistence along the declared path.

For frozen operational boundary \(\beta\), ordinary first crossing is

\[
\tau_\beta^{\times}=\inf\{T:R_T\ge\beta\}.
\]

Each benchmark also froze a persistence functional \(\mathcal P_\beta\). The operational cliff time was

\[
\tau_{\beta,\mathcal P}=\inf\{T\ge\tau_\beta^{\times}:\mathcal P_\beta(R_{T:T+q})=1\}.
\]

Thus cumulative SBT determines first crossing, whereas \(\mathcal P_\beta\) confirms the benchmark-specific persistent event. The operational rules excluded isolated one-window excursions and were fixed before target interpretation.

For probabilistic classifiers, the true-class margin was

\[
m_{i,t}=p_{i,t,y_i}-\max_{k\ne y_i}p_{i,t,k}.
\]

A forward crossing changed from positive to non-positive margin; recovery changed in the opposite direction. A confirmed first crossing was the earliest post-anchor state beginning the benchmark-specific persistence interval. Endpoint persistence was the fraction of incident identities remaining non-positive at the final state. Temporal concentration was summarized by normalized Shannon entropy of the first-crossing histogram and by the largest share of incidents in any three consecutive states.

### Continuous boundary current and observation geometry

Let \(m_W(x,y)\) be the true-class margin, \(\mathcal E_{W,y}=\{x:m_W(x,y)\le0\}\) the error region and \(\mathcal B_{W,y}=\partial\mathcal E_{W,y}\) its boundary. If the class-conditional density satisfies

\[
\partial_t p_{y,t}+\nabla\!\cdot(p_{y,t}v_{y,t})=0,
\]

then class-balanced risk has the conditional current representation [42]

\[
\dot R_W(t)=
-\frac{1}{K}\sum_y
\int_{\mathcal B_{W,y}}
p_{y,t}(x)v_{y,t}(x)^\top n_{W,y}(x)\,dS.
\]

This smooth representation is not required for the discrete identity. The experimental TorchSig, CIFAR-10-C and CURE-OR paths are discrete challenge ladders and are not assumed to satisfy the continuity equation; the surface-current relation is a conditional interpolation and theoretical extension. For local deployment family \(P_u\), risk gradient \(b=\nabla r(0)\) and Hessian \(H=\nabla^2r(0)\) summarize first- and second-order susceptibility. Along a differentiable path, \(\dot r(t)=[b+Hu(t)]^\top\dot u(t)\) plus higher-order remainder.

Outcome-blind batch telemetry was represented locally as

\[
\bar O_n\mid u\sim N(\mu_0+Au,\Sigma/n),
\qquad
Q=A^\top\Sigma^{-1}A.
\]

The matrix \(Q\) describes observable deployment directions and is not part of the formation identity. A direction in \(\ker(A)\) is invisible to the noiseless channel. For quadratic risk, exact constancy on measurement fibres requires \(b^\top n=0\) and \(Hn=0\) for every \(n\in\ker(A)\). Static auditability derivations use classical two-point testing and risk-oriented optimal design [16,21,22]; full derivations and finite-horizon impossibility results are in Supplementary Note 1.

For warning, let \(O_t\) denote current outcome-blind telemetry and let \(S_t=\phi(O_t,O_{t-1})\) be an augmented state that may include a one-step prediction-state transport proxy. Let \(H_{t-1}\) denote earlier telemetry history, \(C_{t,L}^{\mathcal P}\) indicate a persistence-confirmed cliff within horizon \(L\), and \(\mathcal C\) collect the calibration domain, identity anchor, event definition and fixed decision rule. We call \(S_t\) **dynamically sufficient** when

\[
I\!\left(C_{t,L}^{\mathcal P};H_{t-1}\mid S_t,\mathcal C\right)=0.
\]

The empirical nested analysis does not prove this equality; it tests whether an augmented one-step state captures most of the warning information available to the fitted multi-window readout in the frozen CURE-OR field.

### Controlled radio-frequency field and local calibration

The TorchSig task classified BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK and 16QAM signals. Each example contained 512 complex samples. Three controlled impairment coordinates set signal-to-noise ratio, phase noise and amplifier backoff. The baseline classifier was a 240-tree ExtraTrees ensemble with minimum leaf size 2, trained on 2,000 balanced signals. Risk was balanced four-class error.

Each signal was mapped to 48 fixed signal summaries; four class probabilities, predictive entropy and top-two margin formed a 54-dimensional outcome-blind channel. A calibration-only audit froze a 25-dimensional subset containing model outputs, complex moments, autocorrelation and spectral summaries. Local calibration used 125 environments from a \(5^3\) grid with 256 signals per environment. Quadratic ridge regression estimated \(r(u)=\tau+b^\top u+\tfrac12u^\top Hu\). Observation response was \(\mu(u)=\mu_0+Au\), residual covariance used Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage [19], and \(Q=A^\top\Sigma^{-1}A\). Complete feature definitions and stopped qualification stages are in Supplementary Note 2.

### Warning and temporal-order intervention

The principal TorchSig warning source fixed master seed 20260830, batch size 16, six-window history, five-window horizon, 60 replicates for each of two feasible event directions and matched stationary controls. Generalized least squares estimated local displacement, the quadratic risk surface supplied current position and a six-window linear slope supplied the forecast. The alarm threshold equalled the relative-risk boundary plus the calibration-only 90th percentile of absolute cross-validation residuals. A warning was timely at lead one to five; alarms with lead greater than five windows were premature, and any alarm on a non-crossing control was false.

For the temporal-order intervention, each of 120 event replicates contributed the union of values used by the five pre-crossing decisions. We generated 240 permutations per replicate, preserving the exact value multiset and final pre-crossing coordinate. Primary estimands were ordered-minus-shuffled mean slope and forecast. A paired bootstrap resampled event replicates 10,000 times. The counterfactual sequences were not asserted to be physically realizable.

Control-blind charts were fitted on labelled calibration environments but received no named impairment coordinates online. Mean, mean-plus-log-variance, training-relative, oracle-coordinate and wrong-reference variants were inserted into the same warning rule. Complete definitions and results are in Supplementary Note 2.

### Training-support intervention and paired transport

Four equal-size training arms were crossed with five fresh seeds. Each used 2,000 balanced examples and the same ExtraTrees specification. Support-depleted and baseline arms sampled two nested source cubes. Random-broad training replaced 5% of baseline examples with uniform draws from the wider deployment cube. Cliff-aware training replaced the same 100 examples with draws around two frozen deployment paths.

A single 32,000-example calibration cache and 84,000-example deployment cache were replayed through all 20 models. The deployment cache contained two paths, 21 states and 2,000 signals per state. The common boundary 0.1998458772 was inherited from an independent baseline protocol. Primary outcomes were terminal risk and trapezoidal path-risk area. Local \(\tau\), \(b\), \(H\) and fixed-channel \(Q\) were re-estimated for each model.

A second paired experiment used five new training seeds, baseline and Cliff-aware models, 512 latent signals per path and two degradation paths. Symbol draws and stochastic impairments were held fixed across states. A class-conditional velocity placebo permuted one-state margin changes while preserving current margins. Random-boundary placebos drew zero-sum unit normals in four-class probability space and matched anchor error. Paths were averaged within training seed before 20,000 seed-cluster bootstrap resamples. A confirmed first crossing required two consecutive non-positive margins, and boundary locality required the focal forward-flux peak to exceed the 95th percentile of the random-boundary distribution.

### Image-domain formation and repair

Three small convolutional networks were trained from a common stratified set of 20,000 CIFAR-10 images for eight epochs. Each network used four convolutional blocks with channels 32, 64, 96 and 128; the final three convolutions had stride 2, each convolution was followed by batch normalization and ReLU, and adaptive average pooling fed a 128-to-10 linear classifier. Training used cross-entropy, AdamW with learning rate \(10^{-3}\) and weight decay \(10^{-4}\), cosine annealing, batch size 256 and deterministic algorithms.

The frozen networks were evaluated on all 10,000 CIFAR-10-C identities across 15 corruption families and five severities [24,25]. Archive integrity was verified before inference. Risk was misclassification fraction, and each seed used one boundary equal to clean error plus 0.15. Forward and recovery transitions were computed from true-class margins. The fixed incorrect partition assigned each identity a wrong class using a deterministic non-zero random class offset, independent of model and severity. A second post hoc placebo used, for every focal seed and corruption, the trained peer seed with the closest clean risk and compared that peer ledger with the focal model’s risk increments. Corruption families were averaged within each of the three focal seeds before descriptive seed-cluster resampling.

For repair, 2,000 class-stratified identities formed a labelled acquisition panel and 8,000 identities remained sealed for evaluation. Both dangerous arms selected 1,000 ensemble-confirmed first crossings. Hazard selected the deepest negative margins globally; coverage allocated round-robin over corruption family, true class and first-crossing severity, using pressure only within a fragment. A random arm sampled the same field without requiring a crossing, and a baseline arm added no examples. Fresh seeds 71, 83 and 97 retrained the same architecture for eight epochs on the common 20,000 clean identities plus the selected repair set. Every arm was evaluated on the same 15 corruption streams and 8,000 sealed identities. Primary pre-target contrasts compared coverage with hazard on endpoint error and path-risk area; crossing fraction, endpoint net transport and clean error were secondary. The corresponding sparse-field TorchSig repair used five seeds, a 16-example replacement budget and cells defined by true class, path and phase. Full protocols and stopped selectors are in Supplementary Notes 5 and 6.

### Exploratory Covertype analysis

The Covertype analysis was a distribution-level extension rather than a paired replication. The two largest cover classes were partitioned by elevation into balanced 15-m windows with 256 examples per class. Because adjacent windows contained different records, identity-level incident and recovery transitions were undefined. The terminal analysis instead aggregated equal-quantile signed true-margin transport over 45-m transitions, a scale selected after a separate stability audit showed unreliable derivative signs at 15 m. Fit and evaluation panels were independent; uncertainty was clustered over source and training seed. The trained boundary was compared with coordinate-permuted placebos and unsigned hidden-space displacement. Supplementary Note 3 reports complete stopped and terminal results.

### Preregistered CURE-OR v2 confirmation

CURE-OR v2 tested one conjunctive formation–warning–repair claim under a two-phase blind protocol [27,28]. A frozen ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone supplied representations. Five ridge classifier heads used seeds 113, 127, 139, 151 and 163 and were fitted on 150 clean identities. Fifty class-balanced identities were reserved for calibration and 50 disjoint identities for confirmation. This 50-identity unit was fixed by the preregistered class-balanced split, not selected through population-size optimization. Ten challenge families, three asynchronous schedules and 13 states yielded 150 paths; classifier-head seed was the inferential unit.

Risk was misclassification fraction and registered boundary was 0.50. A persistent cliff began at the earliest state with risk at least 0.50 and remained above thereafter. Formation required at least 30 eligible cliffs, at least 30 controls, at least one cliff per seed, closure error no greater than \(10^{-12}\), positive median endpoint change and identity, pairing and phase-order integrity. Warning required pooled timely rate at least 75%, false-alarm rate at most 20%, median lead at least two windows and prespecified per-seed criteria.

Hybrid25 recorded 25 telemetry channels without contemporaneous target labels. The 11 active channels were total departure from each identity’s baseline predicted class; current prediction-margin quantiles at 0.10, 0.50 and 0.90; change-from-baseline margin quantiles at the same levels; one-step net new departure minus return to the baseline prediction; one-step persistent departure; near-boundary occupancy; and mean frozen-representation norm. If \(\widetilde J_t^+\) denotes departure from the baseline predicted class and \(\widetilde J_t^-\) return to it, the online signed proxy is \(\widetilde{\operatorname{SBT}}_t=\widetilde J_t^+-\widetilde J_t^-\). It is identity anchored and outcome blind, but it is not equal to the label-defined \(J_t^+-J_t^-\) in Eq. (1). Fourteen additional diagnostics had zero readout weight. The temporal input concatenated the active vector, its one-state difference, two-state slope and normalized time, yielding 34 inputs. A class-balanced logistic readout with \(C=0.3\), scaler and threshold 0.87 was selected on 100 development paths and frozen before the five confirmation heads.

The repair pool contained 100 family-by-class fragments. Five examples per fragment produced a 500-example budget. A baseline-anchored delta-ridge head with trust penalty 1,000 was deployed only when calibration removed at least one family-level cliff, introduced none, increased no path’s mean risk and achieved aggregate gain of at least 0.05. Otherwise, baseline was retained. Phase 1 committed predictions, telemetry, alarms and repair decisions with confirmation labels unscored; Phase 2 verified the commitment before evaluation. Full criteria and integrity records are in Supplementary Note 7.

### Post hoc comparative analyses on committed outputs

All v7 comparative analyses reused committed predictions, telemetry and fixed identity assignments. No classifier, challenge path, registered boundary or H1–H3 decision was changed.

**Trained peer-boundary placebo.** For each focal CIFAR seed–corruption cell and CURE-OR seed–schedule–family path, we selected the other trained model with closest anchor risk. The peer model’s incident-minus-recovery series was used to reconstruct the focal model’s risk increments. RMSE was computed over adjacent states and summarized over all, active and focal-model cliff paths. To avoid a tautological self-versus-peer pass/fail comparison, peer RMSE was additionally normalized by each focal path’s root-mean-square and mean-absolute risk increment. CIFAR-10-C also retained the prespecified incorrect-label partition, providing complementary task-agnostic and learned-boundary placebos on the same cells; focal self-closure is retained only as the exact zero reference.

**Matched-calibration warning baselines.** Calibration trajectories were reconstructed from frozen ConvNeXt features, registered head-training sets and fixed identity, family and schedule assignments. The analysis questions were specified after confirmation reveal; disjoint confirmation identities were held out from fitting and threshold selection, but not from the choice to conduct these comparisons. For each pre-event window, the binary target indicated whether persistent first passage began within six windows. Logistic regularization was selected by leave-one-family-out calibration. After fitting all calibration identities, the threshold maximized timely warning subject to calibration false-alarm rate no greater than 7.5%, matching the registered 6/81 diagnostic. The fitted rule was applied without confirmation-set tuning to disjoint confirmation identities. Because the specification was written after confirmation reveal, the comparison is post hoc rather than confirmatory. Baselines were normalized time; static current telemetry; the complete 11-channel one-step augmented state; entropy–margin trend; unsigned shift; ridge-calibrated current risk; current risk plus slope; one-sided risk-proxy CUSUM; and a refitted 34-input temporal Hybrid25 model. The CUSUM comparator is deliberately a univariate detector on the calibrated risk proxy and does not represent the strongest possible multivariate change-point method over the full telemetry. The registered detector remained unchanged. A strictly nested ablation independently refitted static telemetry plus net departure minus return, static telemetry plus persistent departure and static telemetry plus both proxy channels under the same calibration procedure. Seed-level summaries treated the five classifier-head seeds as complete clusters. A secondary sensitivity analysis held each fitted score model fixed and recalibrated thresholds on calibration scores at false-alarm budgets of 5%, 7.5%, 10%, 15% and 20%.

**Cliff-difficulty stratification.** Endpoint overshoot was \(R_T-0.50\), endpoint delta was \(R_T-R_0\), initial headroom was \(0.50-R_0\), and onset was the registered persistent-cliff window. Strata were fixed by calibration tertiles before confirmation summaries. We also summarized last-pre-event headroom and two-window pre-event risk slope. These diagnostics compare observed regimes rather than estimate a universal difficulty law.

**Cluster-aware false alarms.** The field rate 3/78 was retained. For model-level uncertainty, the five classifier-head seeds were resampled as complete clusters 20,000 times and pooled false rate recomputed. Paths, families and schedules were not resampled independently. Scripts and row-level tables are released with the v7 diagnostic addendum and summarized in Supplementary Note 8.

### Statistical analysis, integrity and reproducibility

Primary intervention contrasts were defined at training- or classifier-head-seed level. TorchSig path outcomes were averaged within seed before resampling. CIFAR corruption families were averaged within each of three seeds. CURE-OR resampled five classifier-head seeds as complete clusters. Images, paths, families, schedules and windows were not treated as independent model replicates. With only three or five clusters, bootstrap repetition does not create additional information; intervals are descriptive seed-cluster ranges and are interpreted with raw seed directions. Exact one-sided sign tails are descriptive checks, not replacements for broader replication. The CURE-OR field false-alarm fraction is accompanied by a five-seed cluster range rather than a path-level binomial interval. Static binomial risks used Wilson intervals [20] only where the Bernoulli unit matched the reported sampling unit.

Protocols, primary contrasts, stopping rules and invalidation criteria were fixed before designated target runs. Completed negative results retained their original status, including rejected synchronized crossing, measurement-objective superiority and universal coverage-first claims. Implementation aborts before evaluation generation were logged separately and changed no scientific design. Fresh-source streams separated training, calibration, online prediction and outcome reveal; designated TorchSig fits used single-thread execution. Target arrays, alarms, acquisition choices and protocol files were hashed before outcome scoring where applicable. CURE-OR phases were independently audited, and seed-127 abstention remained with zero deployed effect. The v7 fair-baseline, nested-proxy, difficulty, operating-budget and trained-peer analyses are explicitly post hoc and do not modify the preregistered decision. Supplementary Notes 9 and 10 summarize claim scope and the evidence map.
